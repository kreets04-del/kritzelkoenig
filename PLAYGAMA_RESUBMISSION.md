## Longer version

**Kritzelkönig is a social multiplayer drawing-and-guessing game. The actual
content of each match is created live by the players themselves.**

One player chooses one of three words and draws it live. The other players try to
guess the word. The first player to guess correctly becomes the next artist. Roles
change constantly, so everyone draws and everyone guesses. The drawings, the wrong
guesses and the competition are produced by the people in the room — nothing in a
match is authored in advance.

### What was reworked

**Curated visual direction.** The game had the right idea — a chalk-doodle look —
but it was carried by generated raster art in a conflicting style: glossy, beveled,
evenly lit icons on top of a hand-drawn background. Everything visible was redrawn
in one language: monoline strokes, round caps, slightly irregular geometry. The
look of something drawn with a pen, which is what a drawing game should look like.

**Reduced generic AI artwork.** Seven generated images totalling 7.82 MB were
removed: the title logo, the background, four tool icons and the winner graphic.
Five of them were 1024×1024 files displayed at 24 pixels. They were replaced by
about 6 KB of stroke paths inside the document. The package went from 7.83 MB to
0.96 MB and now issues **zero image requests**.

**Manually refined UI.** The title is hand-lettered — a stroke alphabet written for
this game, each letter offset and rotated by a small fixed amount so the word sits
unevenly the way handwriting does. The background is generated at runtime from
twelve hand-drawn doodle motifs on a seeded jittered grid, sized to the actual
viewport, at 5–13 % opacity: the player's drawing is the picture that matters. The
icon set is drawn on a shared 24-unit grid so the pieces read as a family — and the
"clear" icon is a sheet being wiped rather than a waste bin, because you are
clearing a drawing, not deleting a file.

**Improved onboarding.** The start screen now states what the game is in one line —
*Draw. Guess. Become the next artist!* — above the loop in four steps:
Choose → Draw → Guess → Next artist. Available in all five supported languages.

**Stronger social feedback.** The round summary was an icon-and-number string. It
now reads as named lines: the word, the artist, the first correct guess, and the
solve time measured on the server. Event messages were added for becoming the
artist, the ten-second warning, a very fast guess, a last-second guess, and a word
nobody got. They are deliberately not shown every round — something that appears
every time stops being an event.

**Player-created Match Gallery.** A match now ends with thumbnails of the drawings
that were actually made in it, each labelled with the word and the player who drew
it. It runs entirely in the browser from strokes the client already holds — no
server, no storage, no upload — and it exists to make visible what is already true:
this content came from people.

**Curated sound design.** Every effect previously played at the same volume, so the
tick that repeats five times in the closing seconds was as loud as the victory
sound that plays once per match. Levels are now staged by role. A clear warning
lands at exactly ten seconds. The button sound is limited to the actions that
advance the game — not every click needs a sound.

**Hand-tuned gameplay.** Round length, hint timing, speed and difficulty bonuses,
the artist's share of the points, and the badge thresholds were each reviewed and
documented with the reasoning behind them in `PLAYGAMA_CHANGES.md`.

**Improved mobile experience.** The four-step strip collapses and then drops on
short screens so the start button is always reachable without scrolling on a phone
held sideways. Gallery thumbnails scale down and scroll sideways. Verified at
1280×720, 1280×600, 900×560, 740×360 and 375×812.

### On the AI question

We understand the concern and did not try to answer it by producing more content.
No new AI-generated assets were added in this revision. What was added is stroke
paths, CSS and code — and the largest single change was deleting generated artwork
rather than adding anything.

The strength of Kritzelkönig was never its artwork. It is the interaction between
real players. **The players are the content.**
